Why it matters

Most attacks start
with a login.

Attackers rarely need to break in when they can simply sign in. A reused password, a phished account or a former employee who still has access is often all it takes.

Identity and access management (IAM) puts one consistent set of rules in front of every application. You decide who gets in, how they prove who they are, and what they can reach once inside.

  • Password reuseStaff reuse the same password across work and personal accounts, so one leak can expose many systems.
  • Leftover accessPeople change roles or leave, but their accounts stay active in apps nobody remembers to check.
  • Too many adminsShared or over-privileged admin accounts make a single compromised login far more damaging.
  • No audit trailWhen something goes wrong, there is no clear record of who signed in, when, or from where.

Common questions

Questions,
answered.

Something else on your mind? Ask us directly.

Do we have to replace the applications we already use?

No. Applications that support OpenID Connect or SAML can connect directly. For older applications, we review the options with you — sometimes the right answer is to leave a system as it is and protect it in other ways.

Will MFA slow my team down?

Single sign-on usually makes daily work easier: one strong login instead of many passwords. We set MFA rules that balance protection with convenience, so people are not prompted more often than necessary.

Can this work with Google Workspace or Microsoft 365?

Many cloud services, including Google Workspace, support SAML sign-in. For Microsoft 365, we assess whether connecting it to a central identity platform or strengthening its built-in controls is the better fit for you.

What happens when someone leaves the company?

Disabling one central account removes access to every connected application at once. Your offboarding checklist covers anything that sits outside single sign-on.

Where is our identity data stored?

In an environment you control — your own infrastructure or a dedicated cloud instance. We document where it lives and who can administer it.

Why it matters

You can't respond
to what you can't see.

Most businesses already generate the signals that would reveal an attack — failed logins, unusual file access, configuration changes. The problem is that those signals are scattered across dozens of systems and nobody has time to look.

Security visibility brings them together, filters out the noise and highlights the events that need a human decision.

  • Scattered logsEach system keeps its own logs, so connecting the dots during an incident takes days.
  • Alert fatigueToo many low-value alerts train people to ignore the important ones.
  • No big pictureLeadership has no simple way to see whether security is improving.
  • Audit pressureProving controls to customers or auditors means hunting for evidence by hand.

Common questions

Questions,
answered.

Something else on your mind? Ask us directly.

Is this the same as a 24/7 security operations center (SOC)?

Not necessarily. Visibility gives you the platform, dashboards and alerting that a SOC relies on. Monitoring hours and who responds to alerts are agreed during scoping, so expectations are clear from day one.

Which systems can you collect logs from?

Computers and servers through our Endpoint agents, plus firewalls, network devices and many cloud services through standard log forwarding or built-in integrations.

How long are logs kept?

Retention is agreed with you based on your needs, any regulatory requirements and storage cost. We document the retention period for each source.

Who sees the dashboards?

You do. We set up role-based access so technical staff see detailed views and leadership sees a simple summary.

Do we need Endpoint Security as well?

They work best together, because endpoint agents are one of the richest sources of security data. But visibility can also start with the logs your existing systems already produce.
